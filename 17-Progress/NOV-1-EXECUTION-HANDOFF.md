## 1. Mission and boundaries

- The goal is **an internship, not a full-time job**.
- Prefer a genuine, paid technical internship with real tasks, a credible supervisor/team, clear dates and terms, and a schedule compatible with college. Never pay a recruiter/employer to obtain an internship.
- First-year eligibility is a hard filter. A listing saying “student”, “fresher”, “no experience” or “undergraduate” does not automatically mean a first-year student is eligible.
- Big-tech/MANGO companies can remain stretch targets, but the plan must not depend on them. Target roles that accept first-year students: Python/automation, QA/testing, data/analytics, selected backend, AI application/GenAI; treat pure ML research and year-restricted programs as stretch/monitor-only.
- March 31, 2027 is the deadline. Be portfolio/application-ready around **18 January–7 February** if possible; reserve February–March for applications, assessments, interviews, blockers and targeted skill gaps. Do not postpone every application until the full syllabus is completed.
- Exams come first until the end of October. No heavy pre-study sprint before November 1.

## Career-learning tracks

Run two tracks together without letting either become a distraction from the internship target:

- **AI/ML specialization:** Python → SQL/data handling → statistics → EDA → model evaluation → ML → later deep learning/data engineering/AI systems → Kaggle competitions.
- **General engineering:** Git → debugging/testing → DSA → Linux → databases/APIs → software design → communication/interview preparation.
- Python, SQL, Git, debugging and testing serve both tracks. Learn concepts through small implementations, then use them in projects and competitions.

Kaggle is an applied-learning lane, not a replacement for the core sequence. November focuses on fundamentals and competition rules/metric orientation; the first full beginner competition workflow begins only after the learner can inspect data and understand a baseline/validation split. Use the [Kaggle Learning Lab](../19-Kaggle/README.md), [Competition Playbook](../19-Kaggle/COMPETITION-PLAYBOOK.md), [Agent Orchestration](../19-Kaggle/AGENT-ORCHESTRATION.md) and experiment log template.

Agent rule: agents may propose or implement bounded changes, but the learner must run/review code, verify validation and leakage checks, log actual results, and explain the accepted change. Never claim a rank, medal, certificate or score until verified. Do not wait for a Kaggle achievement before applying to eligible internships.

## 4. Execution rules starting 1 November

- **Study capacity:** approximately 2 hours Monday–Friday and 5–6 hours per day on Saturday/Sunday (about 20–22 hours/week). College work and health can reduce this; adjust scope rather than pretending missed hours did not happen.
- **Learn → practise → explain → build:** every topic should lead to a small working file or a clear explanation in your own words. Empty starter files are prompts, not a completion checklist to rush through.
- **Project-first evidence:** finish small usable projects and document what they do, how to run them, limitations, and what you learned. Avoid tutorial clones with no explanation.
- **DSA:** start a small parallel habit from Week 2, about 2–3 hours/week in the first pass. Focus on understanding patterns, not profile streaks.
- **Applications:** start monitoring from November; apply once the first credible work and honest resume exist. Keep checking year-of-study, country, location, work authorisation, duration, stipend, working hours and deadline.
- **Safety:** no fee/deposit for a job or internship, no OTP/password sharing, no suspicious “training fee”, no unverified offer letters, and no misleading claims. Get help evaluating an offer before sharing sensitive documents or accepting.
- **Weekly review:** compare actual progress with deliverables every Sunday. If behind, cut lower-priority topics (advanced RAG, deep learning, CUDA, Kubernetes, full frontend) before sacrificing projects, testing, application quality or the March buffer.
